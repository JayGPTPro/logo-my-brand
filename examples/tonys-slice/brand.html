08 · How we got here

The options you did not pick.

How we got here · the brief

What we read off the business.

Who buys it

Students, families, and locals seeking high-quality, authentic sourdough pizza during the day or late at night.

food: pizza restaurant · mid tier · nocturnal, artisanal, streetwise, unfussy, communal

Where the mark lives

  • pizza box lid: printed, about 250 mm
  • paper delivery bag: printed, about 180 mm
  • greaseproof slice liner: printed, about 40 mm
  • storefront sign: printed, about 1200 mm

Cliches we refused

  • red and green primary color combinations
  • anthropomorphic pizza slices with white gloves
  • bloated 1970s retro display fonts
  • Italian flags and hyper-realistic brick ovens
  • thick bold red sans-serifs inside red circles
  • two-tone warm cream and bright tomato red color blocking
The shelf

Who it stands next to.

The competitors for "pizza delivery Haifa". Colors already taken: red; free: yellow, cyan, blue, purple, pink, orange.

What the shelf does

  • red as a dominant or primary color
  • white or green backgrounds for contrast
  • large, easily readable text
  • simple, iconic logo shapes (circle, stacked text)
  • high color contrast for legibility

The whitespace

  • using a cool color palette (blues, purples) instead of warm reds and greens
  • employing an elegant, thin serif font for a premium feel
  • incorporating a distinct mascot or character into the logo design

What would make us disappear

  • using a predominantly red color scheme
  • using a thick, bold sans-serif font for the main logo text
  • adopting a circular or simple stacked text layout
Where the category is going

Pizza branding is trading traditional pizzeria clichés for a highly playful, nostalgia-driven aesthetic dominated by quirky retro mascots, chunky typography, and warm, muted color palettes.

Read from 21 recent brand identities in the category found on the web.

New brands moved to

  • Bold, chunky, slightly irregular or 'wobbly' typography that feels handcrafted and nostalgic
  • Warm, off-white, cream, or oatmeal base backgrounds replacing pure, stark white
  • Heavy reliance on retro, 'rubber-hose' style anthropomorphic character mascots (walking slices, tomatoes, chefs)
  • Expansion of color palettes into muted, unconventional hues like dusty pink, sage green, and mustard yellow
  • Flat, matte vector illustrations with minimal shading, relying on monoline or bold outlines

Already overused

  • Anthropomorphic pizza slices with white gloves and vintage boots
  • Bloated, highly stylized 1970s retro display fonts
  • The default two-tone color blocking of warm cream paired with bright tomato red

The gap we aimed at

  • Sophisticated minimalism: utilizing elegant, high-contrast serif typography and ample negative space to feel premium instead of playful
  • Utilitarian/Brutalist tech-forward: strict grid layouts, monospaced type, and stark monochrome aesthetics emphasizing modern delivery efficiency
  • Abstract editorial: blending high-end, stylized macro food photography with sharp geometric packaging shapes rather than vector cartoons
