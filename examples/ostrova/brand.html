08 · How we got here

The options you did not pick.

How we got here · the brief

What we read off the product.

Who buys it

Home coffee drinkers and gift buyers choose it for calm daily brewing, countertop display, and ready-to-give occasions.

kitchen: coffee brewing · mid tier · grounded, precise, quiet, tactile, considered

Where the mark lives

  • gift box lid: printed, about 90 mm
  • carafe body front: printed, about 28 mm
  • dripper outer wall: printed, about 24 mm
  • carafe base underside: debossed, about 14 mm
  • thank-you card or hang tag in box: printed, about 85 mm

Cliches we refused

  • generic coffee bean icons or bean-shaped monoline marks
  • steaming cup silhouettes
  • brown-and-cream cafe nostalgia palettes
  • handwritten script paired with slab serif cafe typography
  • mountain badges, stamps, or faux artisan seals
  • literal pour-over dripper outlines as the whole logo
The shelf

Who it stands next to.

The competitors for "ceramic pour over coffee maker". Colors already taken: orange, blue; free: purple, pink, red.

What the shelf does

  • Pure white background packshots with soft shadow or no visible environment.
  • Product-only photography dominates; most tiles show a single dripper centered and large.
  • Ceramic bodies skew heavily white, cream, pale aqua, or hand-glazed blue-purple neutrals.
  • Minimal or invisible branding on the product face; embossing is faint and hard to read on mobile.
  • Ribbed conical dripper interiors are a repeating form cue across many listings.

The whitespace

  • A bold dark background or warm stone backdrop instead of the all-white catalog look.
  • A confident typographic voice in-image: larger, visible wordmark or monogram that can survive mobile scanning.
  • A distinctive symbol language inspired by island, wave, or compass forms rather than generic coffee-bean props.

What would make us disappear

  • Another white ceramic dripper on a pure white background with barely visible embossing.
  • Tiny low-contrast branding that disappears at phone-thumbnail size.
  • Generic aqua or blue glaze without a unique silhouette or graphic cue, because several tiles already live there.
Where the category is going

Coffee-category design is becoming quieter, flatter and more lifestyle-premium, with simple sans-led systems and muted natural color replacing noisy café nostalgia.

Read from 11 recent brand identities in the category found on the web.

New brands moved to

  • Clean sans-serif wordmarks dominate: mostly geometric or neo-grotesk, medium-to-bold weight, with tight-to-moderate tracking; condensed uppercase is common for louder retail applications.
  • Lower complexity logos: 1 simple symbol max, usually a bean/cup/abstract monoline mark paired with a straightforward wordmark rather than ornate crests or illustrated scenes.
  • Muted natural palettes: cream, sand, taupe, warm brown, terracotta, sage/olive, dusty teal; typically 2 to 4 colors rather than broad multicolor systems.
  • High contrast but low noise layouts: large logo centered or left-aligned, generous negative space, sparse front panels, limited secondary copy.
  • Flat matte-looking surfaces and soft tactile cues: uncoated paper feel, kraft/beige substrates, subtle grain, minimal gloss and minimal visual effects.

Already overused

  • Generic bean icons and bean-shaped monoline symbols.
  • Safe beige-brown-olive palettes on kraft-style mockups.
  • Ultra-minimal sans wordmarks with little distinguishing character beyond cleanliness.

The gap we aimed at

  • Own 'measured performance': combine the new wave's calm minimalism with subtle precision cues, grids, calibration marks, numeric hierarchy, engineering-inspired typography, to suit coffee makers specifically.
  • Use a distinctive cooler-neutral palette for the category: graphite, mineral gray, oat, muted steel-blue or soft off-black can separate Ostrova from both old brown coffee cues and trendy beige sameness.
  • Build a stronger product-tech symbol language: not a bean, not a generic cup, an abstract mark drawn from flow, pressure, heat or extraction could bridge appliance credibility and premium lifestyle.
